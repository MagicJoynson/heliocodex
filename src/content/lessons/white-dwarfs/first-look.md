In about 5 billion years the Sun will run out of fuel. It will swell into a red giant, then blow its outer layers away into space. What is left in the middle will be a white dwarf.

A white dwarf is the bare core of a dead star. It is tiny for a star, only about as big as the Earth. But it holds about half as much stuff as the whole Sun, so it is squeezed very tight. A piece the size of a sugar cube would weigh about a tonne.

A white dwarf has no fuel left to burn. It starts out white hot, and from then on it only cools, like a coal taken out of a fire. That takes billions of years.

Any star lighter than about eight Suns ends this way. Only the heavier ones do something else.

The first white dwarf ever found goes round Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky.
