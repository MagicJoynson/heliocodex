About a hundred years ago, astronomers measuring distant galaxies found something nobody expected. Nearly all of them are moving away from us. And the further away a galaxy is, the faster it is going.

This does not mean we are at the centre of anything. Picture a ball of dough full of raisins, rising in the oven. As the dough swells, every raisin moves away from every other raisin. Each one, if it could look, would see all the others leaving. The galaxies are the raisins, and space is the dough.

Space itself is stretching, and carrying the galaxies apart.

If everything is moving apart now, it was closer together before. Follow that back far enough and you reach a time, 13.8 billion years ago, when everything we can see was packed together, unimaginably hot and dense. The universe has been spreading out and cooling ever since. That beginning is called the Big Bang.
