The universe began hot. One second after the start, everything was a soup of light and tiny particles at ten billion degrees.

As space spread out, the soup cooled. Over the next few minutes some of the particles stuck together, and that made the two lightest elements, hydrogen and helium. Nearly all the hydrogen in the universe today, and most of the helium, dates from then. The hydrogen in the water you drink is that old.

For a long time after that, the universe was a glowing fog. Light could not get anywhere, because it kept bumping into loose particles. Then, about 380,000 years after the start, it cooled enough for the first whole atoms to form. The fog cleared, and light could travel freely for the first time.

That light has been travelling ever since, and it is still arriving, from every part of the sky. It is far too faint and stretched out to see with your eyes, but radio telescopes pick it up. It is the oldest light there is, a picture of the universe when it was very young.
