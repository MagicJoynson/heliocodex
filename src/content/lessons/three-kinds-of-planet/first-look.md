The eight planets fall into three groups.

**Rock.** Mercury, Venus, the Earth and Mars are small and made of rock and metal. They have solid ground you could stand on.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/mars-viking-global.jpg" alt="The red-orange globe of Mars with a long dark canyon across its middle." width="1100" height="1100" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption><b>A photograph, stitched together from many.</b> Mars, pieced together from about 1,000 pictures by NASA's Viking 1 orbiter. Solid ground, all the way round. The long scar is the canyon Valles Marineris. Credit: NASA/JPL/USGS.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Gas.** Jupiter and Saturn are enormous and made mostly of hydrogen and helium, the same stuff as the Sun. They have no ground at all. The gas simply gets thicker and hotter the deeper you go.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/jupiter-juno-2019.jpg" alt="Jupiter as a banded ball of cream, tan and white swirling cloud, with the Great Red Spot near its upper right edge." width="900" height="900" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption><b>A photograph, with boosted colours.</b> Jupiter from NASA's Juno spacecraft on 12 February 2019. Everything in view is cloud. The Great Red Spot, at upper right, is a storm wider than the Earth. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/MSSS, processed by Kevin M. Gill (CC BY).</figcaption>
</figure>

**Ice.** Uranus and Neptune are in between. Under a deep atmosphere they are mostly water, ammonia and methane, crushed into a hot, dense fluid.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/neptune-voyager-2.jpg" alt="The deep blue globe of Neptune with a dark oval storm and thin white clouds." width="1100" height="1098" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption><b>A photograph, with chosen colours.</b> Neptune from Voyager 2 in August 1989, the only spacecraft to have been there. The blue is deeper than the eye would see. Credit: NASA/JPL.</figcaption>
</figure>

The groups are in that order outward from the Sun, and that is no accident. Close to the young Sun it was too warm for ice, so there was only a little rock and metal to build with, and the planets there stayed small. Further out there was ice as well, and far more of it. Planets there grew big enough to pull in gas, and became giants.
