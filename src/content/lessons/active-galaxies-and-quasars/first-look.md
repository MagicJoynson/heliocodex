Most galaxies shine with the light of their stars and nothing else. About one in ten is different. Right at its centre is a tiny point that gives out more light than all the rest of the galaxy. These are called active galaxies.

The light does not come from stars. Nearly every large galaxy has a giant black hole at its centre. A black hole gives out no light of its own, but in an active galaxy gas is falling towards it. The gas does not drop straight in. It swirls round in a flat disc, like water round a plughole, and as it is squeezed and rubbed it grows so hot that it glows fiercely.

The brightest active galaxies are called quasars. A quasar can give out thousands of times as much light as our whole galaxy. They are so far away that from the Earth one looks like a faint star, a single point of light.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/quasar-3c-273-hubble.jpg" alt="A brilliant point of light with four spikes, and a faint streak to one side" width="1280" height="1264" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption><b>A photograph.</b> The quasar 3C 273, by the Hubble Space Telescope. It looks like a star, but it is the centre of a galaxy 2.5 billion light years away. The four spikes are made inside the telescope. The faint streak at the upper left is one of its jets, about 200,000 light years long. Credit: ESA/Hubble and NASA, CC BY 4.0.</figcaption>
</figure>

Some active galaxies also fire out two thin jets of fast particles, one each way, reaching far beyond the galaxy itself.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/m87-jet-hubble.jpg" alt="A glowing yellow galaxy centre with a thin blue streak shooting out of it" width="1222" height="1276" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption><b>A photograph.</b> The centre of the galaxy M87, by the Hubble Space Telescope. The blue streak is a jet of particles thrown out from just outside the black hole at nearly the speed of light. Credit: The Hubble Heritage Team (STScI/AURA) and NASA/ESA, CC BY 4.0.</figcaption>
</figure>

Our own galaxy has a giant black hole too, but it is quiet at present. The Milky Way is not an active galaxy.
