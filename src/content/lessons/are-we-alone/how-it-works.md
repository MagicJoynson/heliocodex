This lesson has more open questions than any other on the site. It sets out what is known, what is being done, and how to judge the claims that appear in the news.

**One example.** Everything we know about life comes from a single case. All living things on Earth are related, built from the same few chemical elements, and dependent on liquid water. The oldest widely accepted fossils, layered mats made by microbes, are about 3.5 billion years old, and there are chemical hints older still. So life appeared within the first billion years of a 4.5 billion year old planet. For most of the time since, it has been microbes only. Animals are a late arrival, in the last 600 million years or so.

Two lessons are usually drawn. Life may start readily where conditions allow, though one example cannot prove it. And if life elsewhere is like life here, most of it will be microscopic.

**What to look for.** The search is guided by what life here needs: liquid water, a supply of energy, and the right chemical ingredients, carbon above all. Where all three are found together, a place is called habitable. That means life could survive there. It does not mean anything lives there.

**Where, in the Solar System.**

| Place | Why | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| Mars | Had lakes and rivers more than 3 billion years ago. Now cold and dry, with ice below ground | The Perseverance rover has explored an ancient lake bed since 2021 and has set aside rock samples. In 2024 it found a rock with chemical patterns that on Earth are often, but not always, made by microbes. Settling it needs the samples in a laboratory, and there is at present no agreed plan to bring them back |
| Europa (moon of Jupiter) | A salt water ocean under the ice, holding about twice the water of all the Earth's oceans | Europa Clipper, launched in 2024, arrives in 2030. Europe's Juice mission arrives at Jupiter in 2031 |
| Enceladus (moon of Saturn) | Sprays its ocean into space through cracks. The Cassini spacecraft flew through the spray and found salts, carbon compounds, hydrogen and phosphates | No mission on its way yet |
| Titan (moon of Saturn) | Thick air, lakes of liquid methane, and rich carbon chemistry | Dragonfly, a flying lander, is due to launch in 2028 and arrive in 2034 |

None of these missions is designed to find life outright. They are designed to find out whether each place could support it.

**Round other stars.** No spacecraft can reach another star's planets in any useful time, so the search is done with light. A biosignature is something measurable from far away that would be hard to explain without life. The best example is in our own air. Oxygen and methane destroy one another, so finding both together means something keeps supplying them. On Earth that something is life.

Reading the air of a small rocky planet is at the edge of what the Webb telescope can do, and no biosignature has been confirmed. There have been claims: a gas called phosphine on Venus in 2020, and a sulphur compound on the planet K2-18 b in 2025. Other teams looking at the same or newer data did not agree, and neither claim is accepted.

**Listening.** Since 1960, radio telescopes have listened for signals from other civilisations, a search known as SETI. Nothing confirmed has been heard. The search so far has covered a tiny part of the sky, of the radio dial and of time, so silence does not yet tell us much.

**How to read a headline.** A claim of life elsewhere has to pass several tests. Is the measurement real, and has another instrument repeated it? Could anything that is not alive produce the same result? Volcanoes, sunlight and ordinary chemistry can make many of the same gases and patterns. So far every claim has stopped at one of these steps. Scientists in this field expect the first true discovery to arrive not as one announcement but as years of evidence adding up.

**If the answer is no.** That would be worth knowing too. A galaxy of several hundred billion stars with life on one planet, and a galaxy full of it, are both hard to imagine. At present nothing rules out either.
