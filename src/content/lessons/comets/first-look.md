A comet is a dirty snowball a few kilometres across, about the size of a town.

For most of its life it is far from the Sun, frozen and dark, and far too small to see.

Then its long orbit carries it inwards. As it warms, the ice turns straight to gas and bursts out, carrying dust with it. A glowing cloud grows around the comet, and sunlight sweeps the gas and dust back into a tail.

The tail can be longer than the distance from the Earth to the Sun. All of it comes from a lump you could walk around in a day.

A comet's tail does not trail behind it like smoke from a train. It always points away from the Sun, whichever way the comet is going.

**What it is made of.** Mostly frozen water, with frozen carbon dioxide and other gases, all mixed with dust and grit. The surface is not white like snow. It is coated in dark, tarry dust and is blacker than coal. You would not see a comet at all if it stayed away from the Sun.

**Why it has such a long orbit.** Planets go round the Sun on paths that are nearly circles. A comet's path is a long, thin oval. It spends almost all its time far out in the cold, moving slowly, then swings in fast round the Sun and heads back out. Some come back every few years. Some will not be back for a million.

**The most famous one.** Before 1705 everybody thought a comet came once and was gone. That year Edmond Halley noticed that the bright comets of 1531, 1607 and 1682 had followed almost the same path. He decided they were one comet coming back, and said it would return in 1758. It did, sixteen years after he died, and it has been called Halley's Comet ever since. It is in the Bayeux Tapestry, which shows people pointing up at it in 1066. It was last here in 1986 and is due again in 2061.

**How big can a tail get?** In 1996 a spacecraft flew through the tail of Comet Hyakutake when it was more than 500 million km from the comet itself. That is more than three times the distance from the Earth to the Sun. Yet a tail has almost nothing in it. It is thinner than the best vacuum that can be made in a laboratory. The Earth has passed through comet tails and nobody noticed.

**Comets and us.** Comets carry water and the carbon-rich chemicals that living things are built from. Many astronomers think that comets and asteroids, crashing into the young Earth, brought some of the water now in the sea. How much is still argued about.

**Two common mistakes.** A comet does not streak across the sky. A thing that flashes past in a second is a meteor, a grain of dust burning up in the air. A comet is millions of kilometres away and seems to hang still, moving a little from night to night, sometimes for weeks. And a comet is not on fire. Nothing is burning. The glow is sunlight, bouncing off dust and making the gas shine.
