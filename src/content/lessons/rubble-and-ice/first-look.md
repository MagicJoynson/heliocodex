The Sun has eight planets. It also has millions of smaller things going round it.

They are the leftovers. When the planets were being built, 4.6 billion years ago, not every piece was used. The spare pieces are still out there.

Close to the Sun the leftovers are made of rock and metal. These are the asteroids, and most of them circle in a wide belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Far from the Sun, where it is very cold, the leftovers are made mostly of ice. Beyond Neptune there is a second, much bigger belt of them. Pluto is one.

<figure class="photo">
  <img src="/img/learn/pluto-new-horizons.jpg" alt="Pluto as a red-brown and cream globe with a large pale heart-shaped plain." width="1100" height="1100" loading="lazy" />

</figure>

Now and then one of the icy ones falls in towards the Sun, warms up and grows a tail. Then we call it a comet.
