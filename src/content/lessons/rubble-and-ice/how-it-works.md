**Why there are two kinds.** The young Sun was surrounded by a disc of gas and dust. Close in it was too warm for water to freeze, so only rock and metal could clump together. Further out, beyond what is called the frost line, ice could form as well. That one difference still shows: rocky bodies inside, icy bodies outside.

**The asteroid belt.** Most known asteroids go round the Sun between Mars and Jupiter, from about 2 to 3.3 times the Earth's distance. More than 1.4 million have been found. Jupiter's pull kept stirring this region and stopped the pieces from ever gathering into a planet.

The belt is nothing like the crowded rock fields of films. Put all of it together and it would have only about 3 per cent of the mass of the Moon, and more than a third of that is in one body, the dwarf planet Ceres, 940 km across. The asteroids are typically hundreds of thousands of kilometres apart or more. Every spacecraft sent through the belt has crossed it without trouble.

**Trojans.** Two swarms of asteroids share Jupiter's own orbit, one a sixth of a lap ahead of the planet and one a sixth of a lap behind. The pulls of Jupiter and the Sun combine to hold them there. NASA's Lucy spacecraft, launched in 2021, is due to reach the first of them in 2027.

**Near-Earth asteroids.** Some asteroids have been nudged onto paths that come close to ours. More than 35,000 are known and they are watched carefully. The lesson on falling rocks says more.

**Gaps made by Jupiter.** The asteroids are not spread evenly through the belt. At certain distances there are almost none. Daniel Kirkwood noticed the gaps in 1866 and saw the reason. An asteroid at one of those distances would go round the Sun in a simple fraction of Jupiter's time, and so would meet Jupiter at the same point again and again and be tugged the same way each time, until its orbit was pulled out of shape and it left. The sum for the biggest gap goes like this. Jupiter takes 11.86 years to go round. A third of that is 3.95 years. By Kepler's rule an orbit of 3.95 years lies 2.50 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, and that is where the gap is.

**What they are made of.** Most of this is known from sunlight reflected off them, which shows the minerals on the surface, and from meteorites, which are samples delivered free.

| Kind | Share of known asteroids | What it is like |
|---|---|---|
| Dark, carbon-rich | About three quarters | Like coal-black clay. Holds water locked in its minerals, and carbon compounds. Bennu is one |
| Stony | About a sixth | Rock and some metal, like the stuff of the inner planets |
| Metal | Most of the rest | Mostly iron and nickel, perhaps from the cores of small worlds that were smashed apart |

**Going to look.** Spacecraft have now orbited, landed on, sampled and even moved asteroids.

- *Dawn* went into orbit round Vesta and then Ceres, reaching Ceres in 2015. It was the first craft to orbit two bodies beyond the Earth.
- *OSIRIS-REx* and Japan's two *Hayabusa* craft brought pieces of three asteroids home. The Bennu sample contains water-bearing clays and many of the carbon compounds found in living things.
- *Lucy* passed a small asteroid called Dinkinesh on 1 November 2023 and found that it had a moon, and that the moon was itself two lumps stuck together. Its next visit is on 11 August 2027.
- *Psyche*, launched on 13 October 2023, is on its way to a metal-rich asteroid of the same name and is due there in 2029.
- *DART* was deliberately crashed into a small asteroid moon, Dimorphos, on 26 September 2022, to see whether an asteroid's path can be changed. It can. The lesson on falling rocks tells the story.

**Rubble piles.** Many small asteroids are not solid rock. They are heaps of boulders and gravel held together only by their own very weak gravity, with empty space between the pieces. When OSIRIS-REx touched Bennu to take its sample, the surface gave way like a ball pit, and the arm sank in about half a metre before the craft backed off.

**Where one kind turns into the other.** The line between asteroids and comets is not sharp. A few bodies in the asteroid belt have been seen to grow tails. Some asteroids on comet-like orbits are probably comets that have run out of ice. And Ceres, the largest asteroid, is about a quarter water by weight. "Rock inside, ice outside" is the broad pattern, with a blurred edge.

**Common mistakes.**

- *The asteroid belt is a planet that blew up.* There was never enough material. All of it together would make a body far smaller than the Moon.
- *Pluto was thrown out of the Solar System's list.* It was moved to a different list, along with Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake, when it became clear how many small worlds there are.
- *Asteroids are all grey rock.* Some are nearly pure metal, some are as dark as soot, and some are mostly ice.

**The Kuiper Belt.** Beyond Neptune, from about 30 to 50 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, lies a broad ring of icy bodies. Pluto was the first to be found, in 1930, and the second was not found until 1992. Thousands are known now, and there are thought to be hundreds of thousands more than 100 km across. Four of the five named dwarf planets live out here: Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris. The New Horizons spacecraft flew past Pluto in 2015 and a small Kuiper Belt body, Arrokoth, in 2019.

**The Oort Cloud.** Further still there is thought to be a vast, thin shell of icy bodies all around the Solar System, reaching perhaps a third of the way to the nearest star. Nobody has ever seen it. It is worked out from comets that arrive from every direction on orbits thousands of years long, which must come from somewhere. On this site it is drawn as a faint haze and labelled as theory.

| Where | Distance from the Sun (Earth = 1) | Made of | Seen directly? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asteroid belt | 2 to 3.3 | Rock and metal | Yes |
| Jupiter's Trojans | 5.2 | Dark rock, perhaps ice inside | Yes |
| Kuiper Belt | 30 to 50 | Ice and rock | Yes |
| Oort Cloud | Thousands to perhaps 100,000 | Ice | No |

**Why they matter.** Planets have melted, weathered and remade their rocks many times over. Small bodies have mostly sat unchanged in the cold. They are the original ingredients, and that is why spacecraft have gone to the trouble of bringing pieces of asteroids home.
