Every star in the night sky is a sun. Ours looks different only because it is close.

A star is a huge ball of gas. It is so heavy that its own weight squeezes the middle until it is millions of degrees hot. At that heat the gas starts to burn in a special way, called fusion, and the star shines.

That is the whole difference between a star and a planet. A planet shines by borrowed light. A star makes its own.

Stars come in many sizes. The smallest are dim, red and not much wider than Jupiter. The biggest give out more light than a million Suns.

The Sun is somewhere in the middle, and brighter than most of its neighbours.
