Shrink the Sun to the size of a football and put it on the centre spot of a pitch.

On that scale the Earth is a peppercorn 2 mm wide, about 24 metres away. Jupiter is a large marble 120 metres off, out beyond the stands. Neptune is a pea 700 metres away, somewhere in the streets around the ground.

Everything in between is empty.

Now for the part that is hard to believe. The next football, the nearest star, is not in the next town, or even the next country. On this scale it is about 6,300 km away, roughly as far as London is from Chicago.

That emptiness is what the 3D view on this site is built to show. When you fly from the Earth to the Moon and it takes a moment, then fly on to Neptune and watch the Sun shrink to a bright star, you are seeing the real spacing. Nothing has been squeezed to fit.
