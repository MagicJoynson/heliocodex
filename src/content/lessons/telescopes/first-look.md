Most people think a telescope is for making things look bigger. Its main job is something else: to catch more light.

The opening in your eye is only a few millimetres wide, so it catches very little. A telescope is like a much bigger eye. It gathers light with a wide lens or mirror and squeezes it all into yours. Faint things become bright enough to see.

So the wider a telescope is, the more it can show. The first ones, four hundred years ago, were about as wide as a coin. The biggest today have mirrors wider than a house.

Some telescopes are sent into space, above the air. The air makes stars twinkle, which is pretty but blurs the view. Above it, everything is perfectly sharp.
