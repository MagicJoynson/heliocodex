The Sun is a star. It looks different from the others only because it is so close: the next nearest is about 270,000 times further away.

It is a ball of glowing gas with no solid ground anywhere. What looks like a sharp edge is simply the depth at which the gas becomes too thick to see through.

It is enormous. Its width is 109 Earths side by side, and 1.3 million Earths would fit inside. It holds 99.86% of all the mass in the Solar System. The planets, moons, asteroids and comets together are the other 0.14%.

It shines because of what goes on in the middle. There the gas is so hot and so tightly squeezed that hydrogen is forced together to make helium. Each time, a little mass disappears and comes out as energy. That energy, after a very long journey to the surface, is sunlight.
