Drop a ball and it falls. The same thing that pulls the ball down holds the Moon near the Earth and the Earth near the Sun. It is called gravity.

Everything pulls on everything else. You pull on the Earth and the Earth pulls on you. Two things decide how strong the pull is.

The first is how much stuff there is. The Earth is huge, so its pull is strong. The Moon is smaller, so on the Moon you would weigh only a sixth as much, and could jump six times as high.

The second is distance. The further away you go, the weaker the pull gets. But it never stops completely.

Astronauts in a space station float, yet gravity up there is nearly as strong as on the ground. They float because they and the station are falling together, all the way round the Earth.
