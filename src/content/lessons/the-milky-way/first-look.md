On a clear, dark night far from street lights, you can see a pale, cloudy band stretching across the sky. People long ago called it the Milky Way.

Through a telescope the cloud turns out to be stars, far too many and too faint to pick out by eye. That band is our galaxy, seen from the inside.

The Milky Way is a huge, flat disc of stars, shaped a little like two fried eggs back to back. It holds a few hundred billion stars, and the Sun is one of them. We live inside the disc, about half way out from the middle. When we look along the disc we see the band. When we look up out of it we see only the few stars close by.

The whole disc turns. The Sun and its planets go round the centre at about 230 km every second, and one lap still takes about 230 million years.

Nobody has ever taken a picture of the Milky Way from outside. Nothing we have made has left it, or will for a very long time. Every picture of the whole galaxy is a drawing made from measurements.
