The Sun is a star, and the other stars are suns. They look like tiny points only because they are so far away.

The nearest one is called Proxima Centauri. Its light takes four and a quarter years to get here, so we say it is 4.25 light years away. Sunlight reaches the Earth in about eight minutes.

You might expect the nearest star to be a bright one. It is not. Proxima is a red dwarf, a small, cool star so dim that you need a telescope to see it at all.

Most of the Sun's neighbours are like that. In the list of stars this site draws, there are 108 within 20 light years, and 85 of them are far too faint to see by eye. Only six give out more light than the Sun.

So the night sky gives the wrong idea. The stars you can see are the rare, powerful ones that show up from a long way off. The ordinary stars, the small red ones, are all around us and invisible.

In the 3D view every dot is a real star, placed where a catalogue of measured positions puts it.
