Throw a pebble into a pond and ripples spread across the water. Something like that happens to space itself.

When very heavy things move violently, they send out ripples that stretch and squeeze space as they pass. These are called gravitational waves. They travel at the speed of light.

Albert Einstein worked out that they should exist, a hundred years before anyone caught one. He doubted that anyone ever would, because by the time a wave has crossed space to reach us it is unbelievably faint.

On 14 September 2015 two machines in the United States felt one for the first time. It came from two black holes that had circled each other, closer and closer, and then joined into one. That happened 1.3 billion light years away, so the ripple had been travelling for 1.3 billion years.

Everything people knew about the stars before then came from light. Gravitational waves are a second way to find out what is out there, and they can tell us about things that give out no light at all.
