**It is the difference that counts.** A tide comes from the difference in pull between the near and far sides of a body, and that difference falls away with the cube of the distance, much faster than gravity itself. This is why the Moon beats the Sun. The Sun pulls on the Earth about 180 times harder than the Moon does, but it is 390 times further away, and its tide is a little under half as strong.

**Spring and neap.** At new and full Moon the Sun and Moon pull along the same line and their tides add up. These are spring tides, with the highest highs and lowest lows. At half Moon they pull at right angles and partly cancel, giving the weaker neap tides. The pattern repeats twice in every 29.5 days.

**Timing.** The Moon moves on round its orbit while the Earth turns, so a place on the Earth takes 24 hours 50 minutes to come back under it. High tides are therefore about 12 hours 25 minutes apart and arrive roughly 50 minutes later each day.

**Size.** In mid-ocean the bulge is only about half a metre high. Coasts change everything. Where the sea is funnelled into a narrowing bay the water heaps up: the range reaches about 16 metres in the Bay of Fundy in Canada and about 14 metres in the Severn Estuary.

**The price.** The bulges rub against the sea floor as the Earth turns, and that friction slows the spin. The day gets longer by about 2 thousandths of a second each century. In exchange the Moon is pushed outward. Lasers bounced off mirrors left on its surface by the Apollo crews show it moving away by 3.8 cm a year.

**Locked.** The Earth raises a tide in the solid Moon as well, and long ago that brought the Moon's spin to match its orbit, so the same face always points at us. Almost every large moon in the Solar System has ended up the same way.

**Io.** Io is locked too, but its orbit is kept slightly oval by the regular tugs of its neighbours Europa and Ganymede. So its distance from Jupiter keeps changing, and its tidal bulge keeps rising and falling, by as much as 100 metres of solid rock. The rubbing heats the interior, and Io has hundreds of active volcanoes to show for it.
