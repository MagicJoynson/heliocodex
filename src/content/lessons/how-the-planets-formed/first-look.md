The Sun and its planets were all made together, about 4.6 billion years ago, from one cloud of gas and dust.

The cloud fell in on itself. Most of it went to the middle and became the Sun. The rest could not fall straight in, because the cloud was slowly turning. It spread out into a wide, flat disc going round the new star.

In the disc, grains of dust bumped into each other and stuck. Specks became pebbles. Pebbles became boulders, and boulders became lumps the size of mountains. The biggest lumps pulled in the others with their gravity, and grew into planets.

  <figcaption><b>A real picture, in infrared.</b> A planet caught being made. Credit: ESO/A. Müller et al., CC BY 4.0.</figcaption>
</figure>

That is why the planets all go round the Sun the same way, and nearly in the same flat plane. They are what is left of the disc.
