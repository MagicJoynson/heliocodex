The Sun is not on fire. A fire needs air, and there is none in space. A Sun made of burning coal would have gone out long ago.

The Sun shines because of what happens in its centre. There the gas is squeezed so hard, and is so hot, that tiny particles of hydrogen are forced together to make helium. Each time that happens, a little of their weight disappears and turns into energy.

A little goes a long way. Every second the Sun gets about four million tonnes lighter, and all of that becomes light and heat.

It has been doing this for 4.6 billion years. It is so big that it has hardly noticed.
