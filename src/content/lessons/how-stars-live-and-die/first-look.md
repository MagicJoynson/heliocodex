Stars are not for ever. They are born, they shine for a very long time, and they die.

A star begins as a cold, dark cloud of gas and dust. Gravity pulls part of the cloud into a ball. The ball heats up as it shrinks, and when the middle is hot enough the star switches on.

Then, for most of its life, almost nothing changes. The Sun has shone steadily for about 4.6 billion years and is roughly half way through.

When the fuel in the middle runs low, a star swells into a giant. What happens next depends on its size. A star like the Sun puffs its outer layers gently away and leaves a small, hot ember. A heavy star blows itself apart.
