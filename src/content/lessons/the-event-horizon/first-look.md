A black hole has an edge, called the event horizon. It is not a solid surface. There is nothing there to touch. It is simply the line beyond which there is no way back.

Think of a river running towards a waterfall. Far upstream the water is slow and a swimmer can go wherever they like. Nearer the falls the water runs faster. At some point it runs faster than anyone could ever swim. Nothing marks that point, but once past it, every swimmer goes over.

At the event horizon, space itself is the river, and to get back you would need to move faster than light.

In 2019 astronomers published the first picture of this. It shows a glowing ring of hot gas round a dark middle. The dark middle is the shadow of a black hole 6.5 billion times as heavy as the Sun, in a galaxy 55 million light years away.
