The Sun is a star on its own. A great many stars are not. They come in pairs, each one going round the other, held together by gravity. A pair like this is called a double star, or a binary.

Some families are bigger. There are stars in threes and fours, and astronomers have found as many as seven in a single system.

The nearest stars to the Sun are one of these families. Alpha Centauri looks like one bright star, but it is really two stars close together, with a third, small and faint, circling them a long way out. That third one is Proxima, the nearest star of all.

Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, is a pair as well. Its partner is a white dwarf, the small, dense leftover of a star that has already died. The two go round each other once every 50 years.

Planets can live in these families too. One, called Kepler-16b, goes round two stars at once. From there you would see two suns in the sky.
