Stars are not usually born one at a time. A single cloud of gas makes many stars together, and for a while gravity holds them in a group. A group like this is called a star cluster.

You can see one without a telescope. On a dark night in December, look for a tiny, tight bunch of stars in the constellation Taurus. These are the Pleiades, also called the Seven Sisters. The eye picks out a handful of bright ones. There are really more than a thousand.

Clusters like the Pleiades are loose. Over time their stars wander off and mix in with all the others. The Sun may have started in a cluster like this and left it long ago.

There is a second kind, far bigger. A globular cluster is a ball of many thousands of stars, sometimes millions, packed so close that in a telescope the middle is one solid blaze. These hold together, and they contain some of the oldest stars there are.
