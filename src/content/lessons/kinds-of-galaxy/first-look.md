A galaxy is an island of stars. Ours, the Milky Way, has a few hundred billion. Between galaxies there is almost nothing at all.

A hundred years ago, nobody was sure that other galaxies existed. Today we know there are at least a few hundred billion of them.

They come in three main shapes.

**Spirals** are flat discs with bright arms winding out from the middle, like cream stirred into coffee. The Milky Way is one.

**Ellipticals** are smooth and round or egg-shaped, with no arms. They are mostly old, yellow and red stars.

**Irregulars** have no tidy shape at all. Most are small.

Galaxies are not scattered evenly. They gather in groups and in great clusters of hundreds or thousands. From far enough away, the clusters join up into long threads with huge empty spaces between them, a little like the inside of a sponge.
