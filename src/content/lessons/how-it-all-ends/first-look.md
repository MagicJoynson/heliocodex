Start with the Sun. It is about 4.6 billion years old and in the middle of its life. When it is 10 or 11 billion years old it will swell into a red giant, then puff its outer layers away and leave a small, dim ember called a white dwarf. This real photograph shows a star like the Sun that has already done it. That is the end of our Solar System's daylight, more than 5 billion years from now.

The galaxy will change too. This is Andromeda, the nearest big galaxy, 2.5 million light years away. For years it was thought certain to crash into the Milky Way. In 2025 a team ran the sums again with the newest measurements and found the chance is about even: a 50-50 chance of the two meeting within the next 10 billion years. If they do, hardly any stars will touch, but the two spirals will become one great ball of stars.

Then the lights begin to go out. Stars are made from gas, and the galaxies are slowly using it up. The smallest stars burn so gently that they can last up to about 14 trillion years, a thousand times the present age of the universe. But by about 100 trillion years no new stars are being made anywhere, and the last small ones fade. The sky goes dark for good.

What is left is cold. Dead stars, planets and black holes drift in the dark. On the longest view anyone has worked out, even these do not last. The matter in dead stars may slowly fall apart, and black holes very slowly leak away to nothing. The largest would take a number of years written as 1 followed by a hundred noughts. After that there is only a thin, cold haze, spreading for ever.

That is the ending the measurements so far point to, and it is sometimes called the Big Freeze. It is not the only one on the table. If the push that is speeding up the universe grows stronger with time, it could one day tear apart galaxies, then stars, then atoms: the Big Rip. If the push fades or turns round, gravity could win, and everything could fall back together: the Big Crunch.

Which it will be depends on dark energy, the name for that push, and nobody knows what dark energy is. It was found in 1998 by measuring exploding stars like this one in far galaxies. In 2025 a survey of nearly 15 million galaxies found signs that it may be changing with time. The signs are not yet strong enough to count as a discovery. The end of the universe is still an open question.
