Space is not far away. It starts about 100 km above your head, closer than many towns are to each other.

The hard part is not getting up there. It is going fast enough to stay. To keep circling the Earth without falling back, a spacecraft has to travel at about 28,000 km an hour. That is why rockets are so big: nearly all of a rocket is fuel, burned to reach that speed.

Once it is going fast enough, a spacecraft can switch its engines off. There is no air to slow it down, so it just keeps coasting, for years if need be.

To go to another planet, you do not aim at where the planet is now. You aim at where it will be when you arrive, months later, like throwing a ball to someone who is running.
