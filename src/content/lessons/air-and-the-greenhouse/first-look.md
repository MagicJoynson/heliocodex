Venus, Earth and Mars are neighbours, and all three are made of rock. But you could only live on one of them.

Venus is hot enough to melt lead. Mars is colder than Antarctica. The Earth is in between.

Distance from the Sun is part of the reason, but only part. The bigger reason is air.

Some gases in the air work like a blanket. They let sunshine in and stop some of the warmth from getting back out. This is called the greenhouse effect.

Venus has a blanket that is far too thick. Mars has almost none. The Earth's is just thick enough to keep the oceans from freezing.

**What if there were no air at all?** The Moon shows the answer. It is the same distance from the Sun as we are, but it has no air. In full sunshine its ground reaches about 127 °C, hotter than boiling water. In the dark it falls to about −173 °C. Air does two jobs for the Earth: it keeps in some warmth, and it spreads the warmth round, so that night is only a little colder than day.

**How strong is each blanket?** Without any greenhouse effect, the Earth would average about −18 °C. With it, the average is about 15 °C. So the Earth's blanket is worth about 33 degrees, and that is the difference between a frozen world and a living one. Venus's blanket is worth about 500 degrees. Mars's is worth only a few.

**It is not the amount of air that matters most.** Most of the Earth's air is nitrogen and oxygen, and neither of them traps heat. The work is done by gases that make up a tiny part of the air. Carbon dioxide is only about 4 parts in every 10,000. Water vapour is the other main one. A small amount of the right gas does a great deal.

**The Earth's blanket is getting thicker.** Burning coal, oil and gas puts carbon dioxide into the air faster than plants and the sea can take it out. In September 2026 the air at Mauna Loa in Hawaii, where it has been measured since 1958, held 426 parts of carbon dioxide in every million. Before factories it was about 280. That is half as much again, and the Earth is warming as a result.

**Two common mistakes.** The greenhouse effect is not a bad thing in itself. Without it there would be no liquid water and nobody to worry about it. The trouble is with making it stronger, quickly. And it has nothing to do with the hole in the ozone layer. Ozone high in the air blocks ultraviolet light from the Sun. That is a separate matter, with separate causes.

**Could people live on the others?** Not without bringing everything. On Venus the air would crush and cook a visitor at once. On Mars the air is so thin that water boils away at body temperature, and there is nothing to breathe.
