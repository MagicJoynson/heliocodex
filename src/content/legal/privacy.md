## 4. What we collect and where it comes from

### When you visit the site

You don't have to sign up or give us anything to use Heliocodex. When your browser asks for a page, an image or one of our data files, our web host, Cloudflare, automatically receives and logs some technical information, as any web server does. This usually includes:

- your IP address, which can show your approximate location (country or town, never your street);
- the date and time of the request and the page or file requested;
- your browser type and version, operating system and device type, as your browser reports them;
- the address of the page that linked you here, if your browser shares it;
- security information, such as whether a request looks like it comes from an automated attack.

This comes from your device. We don't combine it with anything else to work out who you are. We don't download, copy or keep these logs ourselves, and we don't use them to follow what individual visitors do.

### If your browser reports a network problem

Cloudflare asks some browsers to send it a short report when a page fails to load because of a network fault. The report contains the address of the page, your IP address, your browser type and what went wrong. It is sent by your browser, not by any script of ours, only when something has failed, and only by browsers that support the feature. It lets Cloudflare find and fix faults on its network. You can't see these reports from our side and we don't use them.

### Counting visits

So that we can see which pages people read and whether the site loads quickly, we use Cloudflare Web Analytics. When a page loads, a small script from Cloudflare (served from static.cloudflareinsights.com) reads timing measurements from your browser and sends Cloudflare a short report. The report says which page was loaded, how long it took, and, from your browser and connection, roughly where in the world you are (the country), what kind of device and browser you use and which page sent you here. Cloudflare says the script does not set cookies or use your browser's storage, that the report doesn't carry your IP address into its records, and that it doesn't track individual visitors across the sites of its customers. We get the totals in a dashboard. We cannot see who any visit was.

We don't use these figures for adverts, to build a profile of anyone or to work out who you are. If you would rather not be counted, you don't have to do anything if your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, because the script is not loaded at all. Otherwise, use the switch on the [cookies and storage page](/cookies), which stops the script loading in that browser. The site works the same either way.

### The sky map, the star pages and "Use where I am"

The sky map at /sky works out which stars, planets and constellations are above you from a place and a time. To do that it needs a place, and you can give it one in four ways:

- By doing nothing. The page makes a first guess at a city from the time zone your device is set to. That guess is made inside your browser. It is not sent anywhere, and nothing is stored for it.
- By choosing a city from the list.
- By typing a latitude and longitude.
- By tapping **Use where I am**. Your browser then asks whether it may share your position with this site. If you say yes, your device gives your position to the page and the page uses it to draw your sky. If you say no, nothing happens and you can choose a city instead.

In every case the position is used inside your browser. We don't receive it, we can't see it and it is not added to any server log, because the star data the page needs is the same for everyone and is downloaded as ordinary files.

When you choose a place yourself, the page remembers it in your browser's local storage, so that the star pages and the birthday-star finder can use it too and so that the sky map is ready next time. What is kept is the name of the place and its latitude and longitude. If you used your device's position, we round it to about 11 km before keeping it, which is more than precise enough for the sky. It stays on your device until you tap **Forget my place** on the sky map, clear this site's data in your browser, or your browser clears it. It is never sent to us. Our [cookies and storage page](/cookies) has the details and the legal basis.

If you type your age into the birthday-star finder on the stars page, it is used by the page to pick a star and is thrown away. It isn't sent anywhere and it isn't kept.

Because we never receive this information, it isn't personal data that we hold, and this policy mostly describes it so that you can see exactly what happens.

## 6. Our legitimate interests

"Legitimate interests" is a lawful basis that lets an organisation use personal data for a genuine reason, as long as that use is necessary and isn't outweighed by your interests, rights and freedoms. Where we rely on it, we have done this balancing exercise. In summary:

- **Running and securing the site.** Every website has to receive basic technical information to send pages to your browser. Using it to spot attacks protects you as well as us. The data is limited, short-lived and not used to identify or profile you. Protecting the security of networks and systems is one of the "recognised legitimate interests" listed in the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- **Answering messages.** If you write to us, you expect a reply. We only use your message for that conversation and the record of it.
- **Keeping the site accurate.** If you tell us about a mistake, you expect us to look at it and perhaps to write back. We keep a note of what was changed and why.
- **Legal claims.** Keeping relevant records for the time a claim could be made protects both sides.

You have the right to object to any use based on legitimate interests (see section 13). If you'd like a copy of our assessment for a particular use, email us.

## 7. Cookies and similar technologies

Cookies are small files a website saves on your device. Similar technologies include local storage, pixels and device fingerprinting. The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR) say a website must ask for your consent before storing or reading anything on your device, unless it's strictly necessary for a service you asked for or falls within another exception listed in the regulations. Since 5 February 2026 those exceptions include one for adapting how a website looks or works to your preferences, as long as you are told and can easily object.

Heliocodex sets no cookies. It keeps up to three small things in your browser, all described in full on the [cookies and storage page](/cookies):

- the place you chose on the sky map, in local storage, so that the sky map and star pages can show your sky; and
- a one-word note in session storage, kept only until you close the tab, if the 3D view's graphics fail on your device and the page has to restart on a different graphics method; and
- a note in local storage that says you have chosen not to be counted, which exists only if you use the switch on the cookies page.

None of them is used for advertising or tracking, and none is sent to us. You can remove the first with **Forget my place** or by clearing this site's data in your browser.

The site also reads a few facts about your device inside your browser, such as the size of the screen, whether you use a touch screen, how much memory and how many processor cores it has, whether it can run WebGPU or WebGL, whether you have asked for reduced motion and the time zone it is set to. They decide how detailed to make the 3D scenes and which city to guess. They stay in your browser.

Visit counting is covered by the exception in PECR for statistics, which came into force on 5 February 2026. It applies where the only purpose is to collect statistics to improve the site, the figures are shared with no one else, visitors are told, and they have a simple, free way to object. This page is the telling, the script doesn't load if your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, and the switch on the cookies page is the way to object. Cloudflare's script uses your browser's performance measurements and, according to Cloudflare, doesn't store anything in your browser. If we ever decide to add anything else that stores or reads information on your device, we'll update this section first and ask for your consent where the law requires it. That is also why there is no cookie banner.

## 10. Data sent outside the UK

Cloudflare runs a global network and is based in the United States, and Google can process data in the United States and other countries. This means personal data may be transferred outside the UK. UK law allows this only where the destination has been found to give adequate protection, or where a safeguard approved by UK law is in place.

- The UK has made "adequacy regulations" for the countries of the European Economic Area and some others, so data can flow there freely.
- For the United States, both companies take part in the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, which the UK recognises as giving adequate protection to data sent to certified companies.
- Both companies' data processing terms also include standard contractual clauses with the UK's International Data Transfer Addendum, which apply where the Data Privacy Framework doesn't.

Because Cloudflare serves pages from the data centre nearest to you, your request will usually be handled close to where you are, wherever that is. If you'd like more detail on the safeguard that applies to a particular transfer, or a copy of the relevant terms, email us.

## 12. How we keep it safe

We use technical and organisational measures that are appropriate to the risk, including:

- serving the site over HTTPS, and redirecting any request made over plain HTTP, so pages can't be read or changed on their way to you;
- collecting nothing from you that we don't need, which is the surest way to keep it safe;
- keeping third-party scripts off the site, apart from the single Cloudflare visit-counting script, which you can switch off, which removes a common route for data leaks;
- limiting access to our mailboxes and accounts to the people who need it;
- keeping passwords in a password manager and never sharing them by email or chat;
- choosing providers that offer strong security commitments and written data processing terms.

No system is completely secure. If a breach of security led to personal data being lost, changed or disclosed, we would assess it straight away. Where the law requires, we'd report it to the Information Commission without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. If the breach was likely to put you at high risk, we'd also tell you directly and explain what you can do to protect yourself.

If you think you've found a security problem with this site, please tell us at hello@fallinghippo.com.

## 14. How to use your rights

Email privacy@fallinghippo.com with "Privacy request" in the subject line, or write to us at our registered office. Tell us which right you want to use and give us enough detail to find the data, such as the email address you wrote from. You don't have to use a particular form or particular words, and you can ask someone else, such as a solicitor or relative, to make the request for you with your written permission.

- **Checking it's you.** We may ask for information to confirm your identity, so we don't give your data to someone else. We'll only ask for what we need.
- **How long it takes.** We'll respond without undue delay and within one month of receiving your request. If we need to confirm your identity or ask you to clarify the request, the month starts when we get your reply. If a request is complex, or you've made several, we can extend this by up to two further months. We'd tell you within the first month and explain why.
- **How we search.** For access requests, we'll make reasonable and proportionate searches for your data, as the law requires.
- **Cost.** It's free. If a request is clearly unfounded or excessive, we can charge a reasonable fee based on our costs or refuse it. We'd explain why and tell you about your right to complain.
- **If we can't do what you ask.** We'll tell you why, unless the law stops us, and tell you how to complain.
- **Server logs.** If you ask about data in Cloudflare's logs, we'll tell you what we can, but we don't hold those logs ourselves and can't search them by visitor. Cloudflare says it has no direct relationship with the people who visit its customers' sites and refers them to the customer, which is us. Please include the date, the page and your IP address if you want us to ask Cloudflare.
