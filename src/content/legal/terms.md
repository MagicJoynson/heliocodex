## 9. The information on the site

**It is for learning.** Heliocodex explains the Sun, the planets, moons, stars and galaxies, and shows them in 3D. We take care over every figure, give a source for it and say whether it was measured, worked out or comes from a model. It is a teaching site, not a scientific journal, and science changes. Moon counts, distances, ages and other figures are revised as new measurements come in, and our pages are only as current as the sources we have used.

**Some of it is drawn, not photographed.** Where a scene, a surface or a figure is a model, an artist's impression or a computer's guess, the site says so. The close-up views of surfaces and cloud tops are generated as you watch, and the constellation lines are one convention for joining the dots. Please don't treat them as photographs or as measurements.

**Positions and times are approximate.** The positions of the planets in the 3D view come from a published approximation that is good to a fraction of a degree between 1800 and 2050. The sky map is worked out from the place and time on your device, which may be wrong or set to a guess. Use it to learn the sky. Don't rely on it to time or point an instrument, to navigate, to plan a launch, a journey or an expedition, or for anything where a wrong answer could cause harm. Use a dedicated almanac or professional software for that.

**Look after your eyes.** Looking at the Sun, with the naked eye for more than a moment or at any time through binoculars, a telescope or a camera without a proper solar filter, can cause permanent blindness. The sky map and the Sun pages show you where the Sun is. They don't tell you it is safe to look.

**It is not advice.** Nothing on the site is professional, educational or any other kind of advice for your particular situation.

We don't promise that any information on the site is accurate, complete or up to date. We will try to correct mistakes when we learn of them.
