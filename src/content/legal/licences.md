## 2. Photographs and images

The photographs on the planet pages are from NASA missions and photographers. Each has its credit under it, and every one is listed with its mission on the [sources](/sources) page.

**How NASA material may be used.** NASA's media guidelines say NASA content is generally not subject to copyright in the United States, and that it may be used for educational or informational purposes, including web pages, as long as NASA is acknowledged as the source. They say it must not be used to suggest that NASA endorses a product, service or organisation, and that NASA's insignia, logotype and seal are not in the public domain and need NASA's approval. JPL's policy asks for a credit such as "Courtesy NASA/JPL-Caltech" unless the caption says otherwise. We follow both: each image carries the credit that came with it, no NASA or JPL logo is used anywhere on the site, and the [terms](/terms) and the foot of every page say that Heliocodex is independent of NASA.

**What we have done to them.** We have resized and compressed the images so that they load quickly. We say in the caption where an image has been computed, coloured or enhanced by someone else, for example the Great Red Spot picture, which is an enhanced colour image made by Gerald Eichstädt from JunoCam data, and the picture of Maat Mons, which is computer-generated from Magellan radar data with the height exaggerated.

**Images that have an owner besides NASA.** Where NASA has used a third party's picture, or a person has processed NASA's data and credited themselves, that person keeps their rights and their credit, and NASA's permission does not pass on theirs. If you want to reuse a photograph from this site, please go to NASA or the source in the credit, check the terms that come with the original and use their copy, not ours.

**People in pictures.** Where people appear in an image, such as the launch photograph, the rights in their likeness aren't covered by the NASA guidelines. Don't use those images commercially without checking.

**Videos.** A few lessons carry a video made by NASA, mostly by its Goddard Space Flight Center and Scientific Visualization Studio, and hosted on NASA's own YouTube channels. Like NASA's photographs, NASA video is generally not subject to copyright in the United States, and its media guidelines apply: NASA is credited beside each video, and nothing here suggests that NASA endorses Heliocodex. The still picture shown before you press play is a frame from the same video. A video plays from YouTube only when you press its Play button; see [cookies and storage](/cookies) for what that does.

## 3. Maps and textures

The maps wrapped round each world in the 3D view are from [Solar System Scope](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/), under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) (CC BY 4.0). Solar System Scope builds them from NASA mission imagery. We changed them: we resized them, converted them to smaller formats and, for Mercury and the Earth's clouds, turned them to greyscale. Under the licence you may share and adapt them, including commercially, if you credit Solar System Scope, link to the licence and say what has been changed. If you take them from us, please do the same, and better still get them from the source.

The Earth-by-night map is built from NASA's Black Marble, and the Earth-by-day map from NASA's Blue Marble, as the [sources](/sources) page explains.

## 5. Data files you may use

The star files that we publish under /data, including the file of star positions, brightness and colours and the file of star details, are made from the HYG database and are shared under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). That means you may copy, share and adapt them, including commercially, if:

- you credit both Heliocodex and the HYG database (David Nash) and link to the licence;
- you say if you have changed them;
- you share anything you build from them on the same licence.

The licence covers the data. It doesn't cover the rest of the site, which stays under section 1. The other files in the data folder are made from the catalogues in the table above and are shared on the terms of those catalogues. If in doubt, ask us.

## 9. How to ask us, or to tell us about a problem

**To ask for permission to reuse our work,** email hello@fallinghippo.com. Tell us what you want to use, where and why.

**To tell us we have used something we shouldn't, or got a credit wrong,** email hello@fallinghippo.com with "Credit or copyright" in the subject. Please tell us:

- who you are and, if you are writing for the owner, how you are connected to them;
- which page and which picture, text or data you mean, with the address;
- what the problem is, and what you would like us to do, such as change a credit or take something down;
- how we can reach you.

We look at every message promptly, normally within 5 working days. Where something is wrongly credited we correct it, and where we've no right to use something we remove it while we sort it out. This page is for matters about material that is on the site. We don't host files or comments from other people.
